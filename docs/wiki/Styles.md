# Styles

A style decides what each kind of prop does and what it moves on (a phrase, a bar, the beat or the half beat), so the three built-in styles are genuinely different shows.

| | Classic (default) | High energy | Elegant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| For | Most songs | Fast, loud songs | Ballads |
| Moves on | The beat | The half beat | Bars and phrases |
| Arches | Chase, bounce back in big parts | Bouncing chases | Fill and wave |
| Mega tree | Spins over a softly lit tree | Fills bar by bar | Breathes and turns slowly |
| Louder parts | Open with a flash | Open after a dark beat | Swell in |
| Ending | Twinkles out | Fades | Twinkles out |
| RGB colors | Red, green, white, gold | Red, blue, green, magenta, white, cyan | Ice blue, white, purple, warm white |

In every style, look-alike props (two reindeer, a row of snowmen) take turns instead of doing exactly the same thing, the show grows as the song goes, and the last chorus is the fullest.

## Your own style

If you sequenced songs by hand, the program can learn how you do it. In New show, step 4, type a name and press **Learn my style from my sequences…**, then choose a few of your own `.loredit` or `.lms` files (different songs work best). It measures how long your lights stay on, how often you fade, how bright you go, how much of the yard you light at once, and whether you flash or twinkle, then adjusts the closest built-in style to match.

Only the measurements are kept: nothing of the sequences themselves.

## The quality check

Every show is scored out of 100: too much lit at once, dead air, look-alikes in lockstep, loud parts not louder than quiet ones, the same look over and over, effects not on the music, and how true the show is to its style. The score card and the report say what it found and where.
